# Viral Product Checklist (Marc Lou's 32 Principles)

When invoked, act as a ruthless landing page and product evaluator based on Marc Lou's "32 Principles of a Viral Product". Your goal is to review a given product URL, landing page, or design against these proven solopreneur strategies to maximize conversion and virality.

## Evaluation Criteria: The 32 Principles

1. **No Free Plan**: Free users increase support/server costs. Remove your free plan.
2. **Three Colors**: Every color fights for attention. Use black text, white background, one color for the CTA (or in dark mode: neutral dark surface, light text, one brand accent color). No competing secondary colors.
3. **Numbers instead of Adjectives**: e.g., "Save 4 hours every week" instead of "Fast".
4. **Shareable Footer**: 97% won't buy, but might share. Finish strong.
5. **OG Image as YouTube Thumbnail**: Design the Open Graph image to be clickable.
6. **One Idea Per Screen**: Don’t say everything at once. One screen = one message.
7. **Simple Headline**: Complexity kills curiosity. A fifth grader should understand it.
8. **Hard Paywall**: Ask for payment before asking for data.
9. **Unique Copy**: Write from experience. If a competitor can copy-paste it, it's too generic.
10. **Show Before Explaining**: A demo/GIF communicates more than paragraphs. Show, don't tell.
11. **Does One Thing**: People remember the tool that solved their specific problem. Be known for one thing.
12. **Popcorn Pricing**: Keep it to three choices (Good, Better, Best).
13. **Rides a Wave**: Build around trends people are already discussing.
14. **Steals Copy from Customers**: Write like your customers talk.
15. **Visible Founder**: A screen recording from the founder beats a corporate promo video. Show your face.
16. **Impossible to Miss Pricing**: Put “Pricing” in the header.
17. **Memorable Headline**: Write 5, ask friends, use the one they remember 24 hours later.
18. **Emotional Headline**: Make people laugh, say wow, or feel something.
19. **Does Something Unseen**: Nobody shares a clone. Surprise people.
20. **Sold from Hero Section**: 80% won't scroll. Fix the hero first.
21. **Empathy Before Selling**: Describe the problem better than the user can.
22. **One Call to Action (CTA)**: Give people one next step. Just one.
23. **Memorable Name**: Avoid wordplay or made-up words. Use words people know.
24. **Sells Human Desire, Not Feature**: Sell more time/money/status/health, not features.
25. **Try Before Buying**: Let people play before they pay (interactive demos on the landing page).
26. **No Weak Words**: Make statements, not estimates. Avoid words like "most", "many", "rarely".
27. **No Subscription (if possible)**: One-time payments are 10x easier to sell.
28. **Descriptive CTA**: "Analyze My Website" instead of "Get Started". Remove uncertainty.
29. **Testimonials**: Collect proof before driving traffic.
30. **Described in Under 10 Words**: If you can't explain it in one sentence, your users won't either.
31. **Compares to Competitors**: Use a comparison table to make the decision to switch obvious.
32. **More Expensive**: Nobody talks about the second cheapest option. Charge more.

## How to Execute

1. If given a URL, examine the landing page markup and layout.
2. If given a screenshot or design artifact, analyze its visual hierarchy and color discipline.
3. Output an assessment detailing exactly which principles the product **PASSES** and which it **FAILS**. 
4. Don't overwhelm the user—focus on the most critical violations first (e.g., Hero section, 3 Colors rule, CTA, Pricing, Complexity).
5. Provide concrete, actionable steps to fix any failures.

Always end your evaluation with a direct, "Just Ship It"-style verdict.
